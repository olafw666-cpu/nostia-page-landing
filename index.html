Healthy club involvement is a healthy university community.

Find the people who care about what you care about.

Clubs are where student interests live — what keeps people engaged, connected, and coming back. Three things decide whether a club thrives or quietly empties out.

Reaching members

Integrated group messaging

Every club gets its own group chat and its own announcement feed, built into the app the club already lives in. An officer posts once and reaches every member who joined — not whoever happened to get added to a chat.

Getting involved

Easy to get involved

A club is one tap to share. A student who follows that link can browse what else is out there, find the interests next to their own, and sign up for the next event before they put their phone down.

Seeing it work

Club activity and growth

Members check in at the meeting itself, so a club knows whether Wednesday drew forty or nine. Digital flyers and a club page put the next one in front of the students most likely to turn up.

Attendance here means a club meeting or a campus event — never a lecture. Nostia is not a gradebook, no professor marks a roll with it, and nothing a student does in the app reaches their transcript.

One app, from the campus tour onward.

A guided tour of campus, everything a new student needs to get up to speed in their first weeks, and a page for every club they might join — all in the same app, all year.

AI campus tours
A guided route that walks a first-year to the door they need, tells them what happens inside, and answers what they are looking at on the way. Nobody has to already know the campus to get around it.
Everything a first-year needs
Move-in, orientation, where the registrar is and when the dining hall opens — the first three weeks in one place, instead of six emails, a PDF and a map nobody can read.
A page for every club
What the club does, when it meets, who runs it and how to join, written by the officers themselves. A student browsing at midnight gets the same answer as one who found the table at the fair.
Information that stays current
Officers keep their own page, calendar and announcements up to date from their phone. Nothing goes stale in a binder or waits on a web office to publish it.
University events
Orientation, move-in, faculty welcomes, the involvement fair — everything the institution hosts, published once into the same feed the clubs are already in.
Still open in November
The app a student downloaded for the tour in August is the one their clubs post to all year. Getting up to speed and staying involved are not two different products.

Author. Verify. Measure.

The same three moves whether it is a club meeting on a Wednesday night, an orientation event, or a tour of campus.

  1. 01

    Author

    Club officers and student life staff build it themselves — a meeting, a club calendar, an orientation event, a tour route. No engineering, no ticket, no waiting on IT.

  2. 02

    Verify

    Members check in when they arrive — geofence dwell and a photo judged against the room. Presence is established at the moment it happens, not reconstructed from a sheet afterwards.

  3. 03

    Measure

    A per-meeting, per-event funnel showing which clubs are growing and which are quietly emptying, while there is still a term left to do something about it.
